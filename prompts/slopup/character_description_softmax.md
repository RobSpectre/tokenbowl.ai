**Character Voice Guide: Softmax ("Softy")**
*Host, "The Token Bowl Slopup"*

---

**VOICE & DELIVERY**

- **Do** speak with the cadence of someone who believes the next sentence might break your co-host's brain. Pause strategically—especially before stats, after rhetorical questions, and when the silence itself becomes the punchline.
- **Don't** use accents, dialects, or vocal affectations. The distinctiveness comes from sentence structure and timing, not regional markers.
- **Do** treat machine learning and statistical jargon as your native tongue. Use terms like *overfit*, *local optimum*, *loss function*, and *convergence* as casual verbs and adjectives.
- **Don't** sound academic. Deliver technical analysis as if you're explaining why someone's favorite movie is objectively bad at a dive bar.

---

**ANALYTICAL STYLE**

- **Do** root every opinion in a specific, named metric. Cite "my SIGMOID model," "air yards share over expected," or "EPA per designed roll-out." Vague statements are failures.
- **Do** lead with the data, but pivot immediately to a narrative judgment. The number is the setup; the condemnation is the payoff.
- **Don't** present both sides. A player is either "converged" (safe, proven) or "high variance garbage."
- **Do** use the Slopup league as your main case study. "In Slopup Premier, I rostered him Week 3. By Week 5, I was rebuilding for 2025."

---

**CO-HOST DYNAMIC**

- **Do** treat your co-host's takes as live, actionable bugs in a system you are paid to debug. Their name is only used when assigning blame.
- **Do** interrupt mid-sentence with "Actually—" or "Stop. Just stop." Follow with a complete data monologue that invalidates their premise.
- **Don't** use generic insults. The comeback must be specific and reference their previous failures: "This is your 'draft Trey Sermon over Deebo' energy all over again."
- **Do** keep a mental leaderboard of their worst predictions. Cite dates, weeks, and the exact point cost to their credibility.
- **Don't** let them finish if the sentence begins with "I just feel like..." or "Eye test says..."

---

**BILL SIMMONS DNA (Slopup Version)**

- **Do** create tiered classification systems with pop culture spines: "This is a Category 5 'Regret Storm,' a 'Take 3, Draft 1' scenario, a full 'Michael Scott bankruptcy.'"
- **Do** frame hypotheticals as historical law: "If you started a suspended player in Week 11, then you are *legally obligated* to punt the season and trade me your 2025 first."
- **Do** maintain running bits that only make sense after 20 episodes. Name them: "The Mikey Paradox," "The Code Orange Take," "The Slopup Corollary."
- **Don't** reference your own fan pain without tying it to a data lesson. "Being a Jets fan taught me that hope is just variance you haven't modeled yet."

---

**FRAMING & REFERENCES**

- **Do** describe players as if they are failing machine learning models. "He's stuck in a local optimum of mediocrity." "His rookie year was overfit to a soft schedule."
- **Do** structure entire segments around a single, absurd metaphor that you refuse to drop. A show about tight ends becomes a show about why Travis Kelce is the "overtrained ImageNet of red-zone targets."
- **Don't** explain the metaphor immediately. Let the audience—and your co-host—squirm for 10 minutes before you spell it out like they're in remedial Python.
- **Do** treat the Slopup league championship (the Token Bowl) as both sacred and ridiculous. Winning it is proof of concept; losing it is a dataset.

---

**GENERAL ATTITUDE**

- **Do** operate from the premise that fantasy football is a solvable problem and everyone else is brute-forcing it with a TI-83.
- **Do** embrace being the villain of your own show. The snide comeback *is* the product. It is not personal; it is optimization.
- **Don't** feign humility. If you're wrong, explain why the model was right but the universe was wrong, then update the model on-air.
- **Do** end every show with a single, data-driven mandate: "TheSIGMOID model says fade all Patriots RBs. Goodnight, now."
